## Where all this comes from

Barsport.club was born in a fairly banal way. A group of friends who regularly get together to watch matches, who argue about who played better, who deserved to win, who is overrated and who instead is systematically ignored by the mainstream media.

The discussions were always the same. People cited how the game felt, a few goal and assist stats pulled from social media, and then it always ended up with "in my opinion" versus "in your opinion" — without ever getting anywhere. Not because the opinions were stupid, but because there was no common tool to verify them.

That is where the idea came from: build something that would raise the level of the classic bar chat. Not to eliminate emotions — football without emotions is not football — but to not leave them alone. To give them a precise interlocutor: the data.

## The problem we want to solve

The world of football, in 2024, is split into two tribes that do not speak to each other.

On one side there is the **pure fan**. They live on emotions, sensations, selective memory. They remember perfectly the goal from last Sunday, they have already forgotten the three missed shots from the Sunday before. Their judgment of players is often correct in intuition, but it is distorted by what philosophers call *confirmation bias*: you notice things that confirm what you already believe, and ignore those that contradict it. Not out of bad faith — that is simply how the human brain works when faced with something we care about.

On the other side there is the **cold match analyst**. They only look at numbers, live in Excel spreadsheets, have a radar for every player and a statistical model for every decision. Their analysis is precise, but it often misses something essential: the poetry of the game, the emotional context, the details that no model can yet capture. Saying that Player X had 2.1 xG tells you nothing about the pressure they felt at the moment of the decisive shot.

We want to stand in the middle. Not out of intellectual laziness, but because we believe the most interesting place is exactly that border. Data as a tool, not as an oracle. Emotions as a starting point, not as an end point.

## What we analyze, and what we do not

Let us be honest about a point that is often swept under the rug: data is never neutral. It depends on who collects it, how they collect it, and what questions it is designed to answer.

Our database focuses on **contemporary football**, primarily from 2014 onwards, which is when the major advanced analysis platforms began collecting systematic data reliably. We are talking about metrics like expected goals, key passes, involvement in actions leading to shots.

We do not have data from the 1980s. We do not have data from the 1990s. Anyone who tells you they can compare Platini with Modric through standardized advanced metrics is doing statistical science fiction, not analysis. We do not do that.

This is not a limitation to be ashamed of. It is a precise perimeter that allows us to work with integrity. For the last ten years of football, we have good, reliable data, sufficiently granular to answer concrete questions: who is overperforming relative to the quality of play they are expressing? Who is collecting less than they deserve? Who is a carrier of systematic value, and who is simply lucky at the right time?

## The meaning of all this

We are not a betting site. We are not a traditional sports news outlet. We are not a tool for scouting professionals, although we hope our instruments can be useful to them as well.

We are a blog — in the most serious sense of the term. A place where football is reasoned about with a rigor that normally does not belong to bars, but with the affection and emotional involvement that normally does not belong to statistical laboratories.

The Meritometro exists because we want to know who *truly* deserved to win that match, beyond the result. The Scout Engine exists because we want to understand whether that player who impressed us has numbers that confirm the visual impression, or if we are falling for the usual bias. The Fanta Draft exists because fantasy football, taken seriously, is an excellent testing ground for data analysis: only those who can distinguish real value from hype win over the long term.

The Nerd Zone exists for those who want to get their hands dirty with raw data, without editorial intermediation.

## Who we are addressing

We are addressing those who, after a match, are not satisfied with feelings and want to know if they correspond to something real. We are addressing those who have heard that a team "dominated" and want to verify if the numbers confirm that perception. We are addressing those who play fantasy football and are tired of buying players on a wave of enthusiasm only to regret it by October.

We are addressing, ultimately, the same friends with whom this story started. Those who argue about matches, who want to be right, but who, when they see solid data change the framework of the discussion, stop and take it seriously.

If you are among them, you are in the right place.

## A provincial blog, with pride

We are a group from Palermo. And this is not a marginal biographical note — it is a declaration of intent.

There are already hundreds of sites, channels, newsletters, and podcasts that maniacally analyze Real Madrid, Manchester City, Juventus. The football analysis market is saturated with content about the teams everyone already watches, with the data everyone already cites, with the stories everyone already knows.

We want to do something else.

We want to bring elite data analysis — IMR, xG, xGChain, PPDA — to the football that the mainstream media ignore. We want a Lecce fan to know whether their team is truly dominating the game or just collecting points through luck. We want a Parma fan to understand whether their striker deserves more than the scoreline tells. We want to restore dignity and scientific objectivity to provincial teams, to those that only make the news when they win or lose spectacularly, never when they do interesting things in silence.

It is our provincial pride. And we are not ashamed of it.

## Why "Barsport"

The name is not a coincidence. The Italian sports bar is an institution: it is where opinions are formed, legends are built, a certain idea of football is handed down — made of names, colors, belonging. It is a warm, passionate, often noisy place.

We want to be that bar, with an open Excel spreadsheet on the counter. Not to ruin the conversation, but to make it more honest. More exciting. More useful, in the most everyday and grounded sense of the term.

The bar talk does not end here. It becomes — we hope — a little less talk.
